Nesting

Use nesting to form a hierarchy that expresses that one element contains or determines the following elements. It is often used to show that some elements are only relevant if a parent element is selected. The two most common forms are disabling and hiding the nested elements.

Radio buttons

Radio buttons allow a selection of one option from a group of mutually exclusive options.

  • Label every group to say what the options are about.
  • Offer two to five choices. For more, consider a drop-down or fewer choices.
  • One option must always be selected.
  • Make the default the first choice if possible. If choices describe a range of impact, order them from greatest to least.
  • Write labels in the imperative, in parallel form and without terminal punctuation.

Checkboxes

A checkbox is used to choose between two opposite states, often to select or deselect items, or to enable or disable actions.

  • Use one checkbox for one binary choice, and group checkboxes to show that choices are related.
  • Nest checkboxes to show that options are only available if the parent is selected. See Nesting.
  • Both the box and its label are click targets.
  • Disabled checkboxes have 40% opacity and cannot be used or focused.
  • Write labels in the imperative, in parallel form and without terminal punctuation.
